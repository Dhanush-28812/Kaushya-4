Team KaushyaMachine lab

Three machines for the cocoon stage of silk. Run each one here.

Silkworms spin cocoons on a mountage. Farmers then harvest them by hand and sort the good from the bad. Team Kaushya, from Wisdom World School, Wakad, is prototyping one low-cost tool for each step. The simulations are simplified models of our designs, not measured results.

Field work behind the designs

  • Visits to KSSRDI (Bangalore) and CSTRI (Mysore)
  • Insight from Dr Sunil Rathode, Scientist B, Central Silk Board
  • Inputs from farmers in Karnataka and Maharashtra, who put hired harvesting labour at about ₹30,000 a year

Aakar: the collapsible chandrika

Hexagonal-cell panels are joined by hinges. Placed on a frame with triangular ridges, they take its zigzag shape and give each worm an equal cell. Off the frame, they fold flat for storage.

Nivara: the scraper harvester

The plastic chandrika rests on triangular ridges so it keeps equal folds. A scraper plate with matching triangles is pushed across by hand and the cocoons drop into the tray.

Vidura: the colour and weight sorter

An air-lock gate lets one cocoon through at a time. Colour sensors and a load cell read it, the ESP32 decides, and the cocoon is routed to the good or bad bin. In this model, a stained shell or a weight under 1.4 g counts as bad.

Sericulture basics

Sericulture is the farming of silkworms (Bombyx mori) for silk. A single cocoon is one continuous filament, so a broken or tangled cocoon loses value. India produces all four commercial silks: mulberry, tasar, eri and muga.

From egg to cocoon

  1. Eggs are incubated for about 10 days.
  2. The larva eats mulberry leaves for about four weeks and moults four times.
  3. Grown worms move to a chandrika and spin for two to three days.
  4. Cocoons are harvested about five to seven days after mounting, before the moth emerges.
  5. Good cocoons are sorted and sent for reeling. A few are kept for seed.

Cocoons that get rejected

  • Double cocoons, from two worms spinning together
  • Stained cocoons
  • Pierced cocoons
  • Thin or flimsy shells
  • Misshapen cocoons
