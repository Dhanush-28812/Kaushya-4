What sericulture is

Sericulture is the farming of silkworms (Bombyx mori) for silk. It combines three skills: growing mulberry, rearing the worms, and processing cocoons into raw silk.

Silk is a protein fibre. Its core is fibroin, held together by a gummy coating called sericin that is removed later, during degumming.

India is the second largest silk producer in the world and the only country that produces all four commercial types: mulberry, tasar, eri and muga. Mulberry silk makes up most of the output, and Karnataka leads in it.

Most rearing is done by small farmers at home, which is why a cheap, simple tool matters more than an expensive machine.

What makes a good cocoon

Reelers pay by quality, so sorting decides how much the farmer earns. Graders look for these faults:

Double cocoon
Two worms spun together. The tangled thread cannot be reeled as a clean filament.
Stained cocoon
Discoloured by a dead worm or moisture. It lowers the colour and grade of the silk.
Pierced cocoon
Damaged by a pest or an emerged moth. The continuous thread is broken.
Thin or flimsy shell
Too little silk, so poor reeling yield.
Malformed cocoon
Irregular size or shape, often caused by crowded or uneven mounting.

Uniform cocoons start at mounting. That is why our work begins with the chandrika, not the sorter.

Our three tools

One tool for each stage. All three are in the prototyping phase.

Aakar

Mounting

A collapsible plastic chandrika that keeps the familiar hexagonal cell shape. Honeycomb panels are joined by hinges and sit on a frame with triangular ridges. Taken off the frame, it folds flat for storage. The aim is uniform cocoon size and shape.

Nivara

Harvesting

The plastic chandrika rests on a frame with triangular ridges so it takes equal folds. A matching panel presses it down from above, and a scraper plate with matching triangles is pushed across to lift the cocoons off into a collection tray. Hired harvesting labour costs farmers about ₹30,000 a year, based on what they told us.

Vidura

Sorting

A sensor-based sorter. A hopper and air-lock gate release one cocoon at a time. Three TCS34725 colour sensors and an HX711 load cell read colour and weight, an ESP32 decides, and the cocoon is routed into a good or bad bin.

What we learned in the field

  • Visited KSSRDI, Bangalore, and CSTRI, Mysore.
  • Spoke with Dr Sunil Rathode, Scientist B, Central Silk Board, Bangalore.
  • Collected inputs from farmers in Karnataka and Maharashtra.
  • Reworked Nivara after learning that the pin-and-plate harvesters built for square-cell rotary mountages cannot work on a folding hexagonal chandrika.
